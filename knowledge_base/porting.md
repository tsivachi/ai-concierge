# Transferring (Porting) Your Number

Porting moves your existing phone number from your old carrier to your new line, so you keep the same number your contacts already have.

A port typically takes anywhere from a few minutes to a few hours, though it can occasionally take longer if your old carrier requires additional verification. During the port, your old service may briefly stop working before the new line takes over — this is normal and expected.

To avoid delays, make sure the account details you provided (old account number, PIN, and billing zip code) exactly match what's on file with your previous carrier; mismatched details are the single most common cause of a port getting stuck in a pending state. If your port has been pending for more than a day, it's worth double-checking those details or asking the previous carrier if a port-out PIN or freeze is blocking the release.

If a port fails, you can typically retry it — most failures are caused by incorrect account information rather than a permanent problem. New lines that don't need to keep an existing number don't need to go through porting at all; they can activate immediately with a new number.
