# Understanding Your Postpaid Bill

A postpaid bill is made up of a few distinct pieces: your recurring plan charge, any one-time charges (like an activation fee), a device installment if you financed a phone, taxes and regulatory fees, and any promotional credits that reduce the total.

Your bill cycle has a start and end date — charges are billed for the upcoming cycle, so your first bill is often larger than later ones because it may include a partial month plus setup charges. This is normal and expected, not an error.

If a charge looks unfamiliar, check the itemized breakdown first — most "surprise" charges turn out to be taxes/fees or a prorated first-cycle charge. If you believe a specific charge is genuinely wrong or unauthorized (not just unfamiliar), that's a billing dispute and should be escalated to a human agent so it can be investigated and corrected.

AutoPay and paperless billing don't change what you owe, but AutoPay can help you avoid a missed-payment fee, and many plans offer a small discount for enrolling.
